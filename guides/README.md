# Yardplan guides — irrigation & maintenance (standalone modules)

These modules take a Yardplan design and produce an irrigation plan and a maintenance calendar from it. They don't change `/workspace/yardplan`: they read `../yardplan/data.js` (the plant catalog), reuse the contractor price book (`createPriceBook({ items })`) and the vendored jsPDF in `../yardplan-contractor/vendor/`.

> **Disclaimer:** This is a general planning guide, not an engineered design. Measure flow and pressure on site, and follow local plumbing codes, permits and water-use rules. **Backflow preventers often have to be installed by a licensed installer and tested every year.** The climate data, ETo and watering rules are estimates or unverified notes (see below).

| `ics.js` | `buildICS(guide)`: RFC 5545 calendar (yearly RRULE, 1-day alarm, folded CRLF lines) |

| `catalog.js` / `catalog-node.js` | Read the app catalog in the browser (`catalogFromGlobals()`) or in Node (`loadAppCatalog()`, uses vm, read-only) |

| `demo.html` | Demo (serve `/workspace` over HTTP, open `/yardplan-guides/demo.html`; `?tab=maintenance`, `?selftest=1`) |

## API
```js
import { generateIrrigationPlan } from './irrigation.js';
import { generateMaintenanceGuide } from './maintenance.js';
const plan = generateIrrigationPlan(design, { zip: '89501', catalog, waterSource: 'municipal'|'well', pressurePsi, flowGpm,
  lawnType?: 'cool'|'warm', manifold?: {x,z}, consolidate?: true });
// plan.hydrozones[]: { id, name, method: 'drip-point'|'inline-drip'|'mp-rotator', water, exposure, trees, plantFactor, efficiency, color,
//   plants[{id,cls,emitters,gph,container}], emitterCount, headCount, totalGph, gpm, valves, irrigatedSqft,
//   tubing{halfInchFt,quarterInchFt,inlineFt,lateralPipeFt}, monthly[12]{depthIn,gallons,off,winterized?,intervalDays?,eventsPerWeek,minutesPerEvent,cycleSoak?},
//   establishment[], annualGallons, overlay{outline,emitters,heads} }
// plan.valveCount/dripValves/lawnValves/maxValveGpm/withinFlowBudget, plan.supply, plan.equipment{controller,backflow,pressureRegulation,filter},
// plan.parts[] (key,name,unit,unitCost,qty,extended), plan.priceBookItems (→ createPriceBook({items})), plan.takeoffLines (incl. contractor 'drip' key),
// plan.waterUse{annualGallons,monthlyGallons,baselineAnnualGallons,savingsGallons,savingsPct,method}, plan.overlay{manifold,zones[]},
// plan.location, plan.warnings, plan.notes, plan.disclaimer
const guide = generateMaintenanceGuide(design, { zip, catalog, irrigationPlan?: plan, proRate?: 65 });
// guide.tasks[] {id,category,title,months[],recurrence,minutes,diyCost,proCost,details,date?}, guide.months[12]{month,name,tasks[ids]},
// guide.annual{hours,diyCost,proCost}, guide.score{level:'easy'|'moderate'|'high',points,breakdown}, guide.freezeClimate, guide.frostFree
```
Overlay coordinates use the same frame as yard3d: feet, origin at the yard center, x/z, matching `items[].x/z`. For the 3D view, place small markers at `plan.overlay.zones[].emitters` and `.heads` (radius = throw), and draw `outline` as a ground polygon in `color`.

## Method & assumptions
- **Hydrozones:** grouped by water need (catalog `water`: low/med/high) and sun (`full` in the plant's sun list = sun, otherwise shade). Trees get their own zones by water need. Drip-point zones with fewer than 3 plants merge into the same-water zone with the other exposure (`consolidate:false` turns this off). Lawn is a separate MP-rotator zone. Gravel, paths, DG and walls get no irrigation, but plants inside gravel still get drip.
- **Emitters (PC):** trees get a ring of 4–8 × 2 GPH on an ~8 ft young-tree root zone (expand it as the tree grows). Large shrubs 2 × 2 GPH, small shrubs 2 × 1, large perennials/grasses 1 × 2, small ones 1 × 1, groundcovers 0.5, succulents 0.5–1. Dense beds (mulch bed, ≥6 non-tree plants of one water class, canopy ≥50% of the bed) get 0.6 GPH inline dripline at 12″, rows 18″ apart.
- **Lawn:** MP-rotator grid at ≤15 ft spacing (90/180/360° arcs), ~0.4 in/hr. Cool-season PF 0.8, warm-season 0.6 (warm if zone ≥8 and summers aren't mild).
- **Plant factors (WUCOLS-style midpoints):** low 0.2, moderate 0.5, high 0.8 (ranges reported). Efficiency: drip 0.9, MP 0.75, baseline spray 0.65.
- **Runtimes:** monthly depth = max(0, ETo×PF − 0.5×rain) / efficiency. Drip gallons = depth × wetted area × 0.623, and minutes = gallons × interval ÷ zone GPH × 60. The interval scales with ETo relative to peak (low 7 d, med 4 d, high 2 d at peak; trees 2×). Lawn uses 1–3 days/week, capped by the verified local spray-day limit for that month (drip too where the utility limits it: SNWA all year, Austin 2 days), with cycle-soak above 30 min. In freeze climates the system is treated as winterized from first frost until a month before last frost.
- **Establishment:** drip runs daily for weeks 1–2, then every 2–3 days, then every 3–5 days. Runtime is set by the gallons needed to re-wet the root ball for each container size. Lawn has separate seed and sod schedules. Trees keep weekly deep soaks for 1–2 summers.
- **Flow budget:** design to 75% of available flow, assumed at 12 GPM municipal or 8 GPM well when not measured. Each drip circuit is capped at 240 GPH (½″ poly). Valves = max(flow split, circuit split). Controller stations = next size ≥ valves + 1.
- **Water use baseline:** the whole yard minus paths and walls, treated as lawn on standard sprays.
- **Parts costs:** example 2026 retail ballpark figures. Override them in the price book.
- **Maintenance:** timing comes from frost dates (late-winter pruning about 2 months before last frost, startup at last frost, blowout a week before first frost) and from monthly mean temperature (pre-emergent at mean ≥50°F, fall pre-emergent when the mean drops below 70°F). Species rules cover oak wilt, maple/birch sap bleed, bloom on old vs new wood, boxwood shearing and so on. Pro cost uses an example rate of $65/h. The score adds points for lawn area, hedges, perennial count, high-water plants, trees, hardscape and annual hours, and subtracts for low-water plants.

## Data sources (cite; estimates marked)
- **ZIP → lat/lon:** US Census Bureau 2023 Gazetteer, ZCTA national file (public domain), https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/gazetteer/2023_Gazetteer/2023_Gaz_zcta_national.zip. State comes from USPS 3-digit prefix ranges.
- **Climate normals and frost dates:** downloaded from NOAA NCEI U.S. Climate Normals 1991–2020 v1.0.1 (public domain), by-station multivariate archives:
  - monthly: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/normals-monthly/1991-2020/archive/us-climate-normals_1991-2020_v1.0.1_monthly_multivariate_by-station_c20230404.tar.gz (`MLY-TMAX-NORMAL`, `MLY-TMIN-NORMAL`, `MLY-PRCP-NORMAL`)
  - annual/seasonal: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/normals-annualseasonal/1991-2020/archive/us-climate-normals_1991-2020_v1.0.1_annualseasonal_multivariate_by-station_c20230404.tar.gz (`ANN-TMIN-PRBLST-T32FP50` / `ANN-TMIN-PRBFST-T32FP50` = 50% last/first 32°F dates, `ANN-TMIN-AVGNDS-LSTH032`)
  - `tools/build-normals.cjs <monthlyDir> <annualDir>` rebuilds `data/climate-stations.js`. All 15,616 stations were scanned and the 6,719 with complete 12-month Tmax, Tmin and precipitation are kept, covering all 50 states, DC, PR and the territories. 6,402 of them have 50% frost dates. Each row stores its GHCN-D station ID (e.g. Reno sample → `USC00266791` RENO WFO). `META` in the file records the URLs, download date and counts. No date means 32°F happens in fewer than half of years, which is treated as frost-free.
  - Each ZIP uses the nearest station. `station.distanceMi` and `confidence` report the distance; elevation differences in mountain areas are not corrected.
- **ETo:** Hargreaves–Samani 1985 equation, with Ra from latitude per FAO-56 (Allen et al. 1998, eqs. 21 and 52; tested against FAO-56 Example 8), computed from the real NOAA Tmax/Tmin normals. Still an **estimate**: it tends to read low in windy arid places and high on the humid coast.
- **USDA zone:** the normals don't include the annual extreme minimum. Order of preference: the app lookup (`opts.zone` / `location`), then the app's `ZIP_FALLBACK`, then an **estimate** (coldest-month mean min − 20°F).
- **Water rules:** checked against official utility pages on **2026-10-13** (URLs are in `data/restrictions.js` → `sources`):
  - TMWA: tmwa.com assigned-day pages and FAQ
  - SNWA/LVVWD: snwa.com irrigation-clock and how-to-water pages; lvvwd.com schedule
  - Phoenix: phoenix.gov Drought Dashboard. Stage 1 Water Alert, no day schedule. The AMWUA guideline reference is not separately verified.
  - Denver Water: summer watering rules page, Resolution 002-2026, and the Oct 1 lawn-ban article. Currently a Stage 1 lawn/spray ban.
  - LADWP: ordinance page and FAQ. Phase 2.
  - Austin Water: find-your-watering-day page, drought response page and Code §6-4-30. Conservation Stage.
  - All six are `verified:true` as of that date. They change with drought stages.
- **Plant factors:** WUCOLS IV (UC Davis) categories. **Turf coefficients and efficiencies:** common ET-irrigation planning values (Irrigation Association, EPA WaterSense guidance).

## Tests
`npm test` runs 8 tests: location, hydrozoning, runtime math, flow budget, water savings plus price book, frost vs. no-frost calendar (Reno vs. LA), ICS format, and a PDF smoke test. The headless Chrome check is `demo.html?selftest=1`; screenshots are in `screenshots/`.

## California default, restrictions and MWELO (2026-10-13)
- Default sample: **Sacramento 95814** (`SACRAMENTO_DESIGN`, `DEFAULT_ZIP`). Reno 89501 is still available as an alternate: pick it in the demo or use `?sample=reno`.
- `data/restrictions.js` has 44 entries, each with `sources`, `checked` and `verified`. CA: 16 verified, 2 unverified. Other states: 24 verified, 2 unverified. There are state layers for CA (23 CCR §995 + MWELO + Conservation Way of Life + AB 1572) and GA. All 50 states + DC + PR have a default entry ("Check your local water district"); these defaults are unverified. Matching order: the smallest-radius utility first, then the county (`data/zip-county.js`, Census 2020 ZCTA–county), then the state default.
- `mwelo.js`: for CA plans, `plan.mwelo` compares MAWA = (ETo−Eppt)×0.62×(ETAF×LA + SLA) against ETWU = Σ ETo×0.62×PF×HA/IE. ETAF is 0.55 residential and 0.45 non-residential. The permit thresholds are 500 sq ft new and 2,500 sq ft rehab. It also gives hints on 25% turf, 75% low-PF planting, no lawn strips narrower than 10 ft, and a 24 in. overhead setback.
- Zones come from `data/usda-zones.js` (PRISM/OSU 2023 phzm ZIP CSVs, 40,502 ZIPs). Otherwise they come from the app's ZIP_FALLBACK, and failing that from the NOAA estimate.
- This is not legal advice. Watering rules change, so check with your water supplier.
